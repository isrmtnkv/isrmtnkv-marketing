Refresh content when the answer changes, not just the date

Changing a publication date can make a page look current without making it useful. A meaningful refresh starts with what the reader can no longer rely on.

The short answer

Review claims, examples, links and instructions against the current task. Update the substance and explain important limitations where facts remain uncertain.

Before you start

  • Define the reader’s task and the distinct question the page is intended to answer.
  • Inventory related URLs, page metadata and existing content before creating another similar page.
  • Identify which claims need current sources and who will keep the answer accurate over time.

Put it into practice

01Define the starting point

Prioritize change-sensitive topics. Platform availability, interface workflows and integration methods can age faster than basic economic formulas. Give them a review cadence proportionate to the risk of becoming misleading.

02Build the next step

Use reader evidence. Search queries, support questions and sales feedback can reveal missing explanations or outdated assumptions. Add the answer that helps the reader progress rather than padding the page.

03Review the evidence

Preserve useful history when appropriate. If a recommendation changed, explain the new condition instead of silently presenting a previous assumption as timeless truth.

A worked example

ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO · NOT A CLIENT RESULT

Illustrative example: an advertising platform expands eligibility. A useful update revises the access guidance and points readers to the official current list; changing only the date would leave the old restriction misleading.

A mistake to avoid

Avoid expanding every article simply to increase word count. Additional detail is valuable when it answers a question, supplies evidence or prevents a mistake.

How to evaluate the result

Read the page as someone who wants to solve the problem, not as someone counting keywords. Check that the answer is direct, the examples are honest and the next action is usable. Then verify the technical context: accessibility, internal links, preferred URL, language and indexing signals. Search discovery and commercial outcomes take observation time; avoid making immediate ranking promises from publication alone. Review usefulness and maintenance cost together, and consolidate overlapping pages when a single stronger answer would serve readers better.

Apply this to your business

Maintain a review queue with topic, change-sensitive claims, source, last verification, reader gaps and the specific revision needed. Mark pages that require fresh confirmation before reuse.

Your working notes

Copy these prompts into your project document. Keep observations separate from assumptions and give the next action an owner.

  1. Reader task, main query and unique contribution
  2. Evidence, example and practical next action
  3. Technical checks, related links and review date

Record what happened at the next review. If the evidence is incomplete, write down what would resolve the uncertainty instead of forcing a positive or negative verdict.
