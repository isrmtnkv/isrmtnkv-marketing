Choose marketing channels around the buying journey

A competitor’s channel mix is not automatically the right starting point for your business. Offer complexity, customer behavior and operational capacity change what makes sense.

The short answer

Give each channel a defined role and test the smallest useful plan. Select based on fit and evidence rather than trying to appear everywhere.

Before you start

  • Write down the product, ideal customer, target markets and delivery constraints without marketing jargon.
  • Collect recent sales outcomes, objections and the economics the acquisition plan must respect.
  • Identify who can approve changes and who owns each step after an enquiry arrives.

Put it into practice

01Define the starting point

Map where customers express demand, discover alternatives and seek reassurance. Search, social feeds, communities and AI-assisted exploration can play different roles. Look for a credible connection between context and your offer.

02Build the next step

Assess the assets each channel needs. Search requires demand and relevant destinations; visual discovery needs creative production; community journeys need useful content and follow-up. Include these costs in the decision.

03Review the evidence

Prioritize one channel with a plausible path to qualified demand. Add another when it answers a specific question or reduces a demonstrated dependency, not simply because the first campaign feels slow.

A worked example

ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO · NOT A CLIENT RESULT

Illustrative example: a specialist repair service may start with explicit local search demand. A new category with little existing search interest may need stronger educational creative before buyers know what to ask for.

A mistake to avoid

Avoid ranking channels only by raw CPL. Quality, close rate, sales effort and time to revenue can reverse the apparent ranking.

How to evaluate the result

A useful strategy should change a concrete decision: which customer to prioritize, what to offer, where to invest or which bottleneck to fix. Test the plan against recent real enquiries and the team’s operating capacity. If several plausible explanations remain, record the uncertainty and choose an experiment that separates them. Review the assumptions when the offer, market or sales process changes. Keep the plan short enough that the people executing it can explain the priorities without reopening a large presentation.

Apply this to your business

Create a shortlist with buyer behavior, required assets, measurement limits, expected learning and operational cost. Choose a first test and write down why the alternatives are deferred.

Your working notes

Copy these prompts into your project document. Keep observations separate from assumptions and give the next action an owner.

  1. Customer situation and primary business constraint
  2. Chosen action and reason to defer alternatives
  3. Evidence, accountable owner and decision checkpoint

Record what happened at the next review. If the evidence is incomplete, write down what would resolve the uncertainty instead of forcing a positive or negative verdict.
