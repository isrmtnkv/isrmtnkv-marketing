Marketing attribution: use models as lenses, not verdicts

Customers often encounter several channels before enquiring. Assigning credit is a modeling choice, and different models can tell different stories about the same journey.

The short answer

Use attribution to inform decisions while acknowledging missing information. No single model perfectly reconstructs every influence on a purchase.

Before you start

  • Identify the authoritative source for spend, contacts, qualification and customer outcomes.
  • Document metric definitions, date basis, duplicate rules and reporting delays before comparing totals.
  • Select a small set of traceable records to verify the journey across systems.

Put it into practice

01Define the starting point

Clarify what the report measures. Interaction date, conversion date, reporting window and identity resolution all affect the result. Compare models only after understanding these definitions.

02Build the next step

Separate attribution from incrementality. A channel can appear in a journey without being necessary to the sale. Estimating what would happen without the activity requires a more deliberate experimental design.

03Review the evidence

Triangulate evidence. Use CRM sources, self-reported discovery, channel reports and appropriate controlled tests. Disagreement can reveal a measurement limitation rather than a reason to select the most flattering number.

A worked example

ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO · NOT A CLIENT RESULT

Illustrative example: a prospect discovers a service on Facebook, reads a guide and later searches the brand on Google. Last-click reporting may credit search while obscuring the earlier discovery.

A mistake to avoid

Do not add attributed sales from several ad platforms and present the sum as unique revenue. Overlapping credit can exceed actual business sales.

How to evaluate the result

Reconcile differences before choosing the most favorable number. Systems can count events, people and opportunities differently, and each report can use a different date or attribution rule. Label incomplete cohorts, unavailable data and known blind spots explicitly. Review whether a change in the metric comes from customer behavior or from a revised definition or integration. Once the measurement is reliable enough, use it to support a decision and name the uncertainty that remains. Precision in formatting should never exceed precision in the underlying evidence.

Apply this to your business

Write a short interpretation guide for each report: what it credits, what it misses, which decision it supports and which claim it cannot justify.

Your working notes

Copy these prompts into your project document. Keep observations separate from assumptions and give the next action an owner.

  1. Metric formula, scope and authoritative source
  2. Known gaps, duplicates and timing differences
  3. Decision supported and evidence still missing

Record what happened at the next review. If the evidence is incomplete, write down what would resolve the uncertainty instead of forcing a positive or negative verdict.
